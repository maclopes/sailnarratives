
# From text to minigame: a method for the Tagus boats game

2026-11-11 ·


## The method at a glance

Define the foundations once: the sailing concepts, the areas of sailing, and the boats with their functions. Then every historical text becomes a minigame in four steps: pull out the passages that describe an action, link each to one sailing concept, build the minigame around that sequence, and present it with a short companion text.

1. **Extract.** Mark the actions, places, conditions and numbers in the source.
2. **Map.** Link each action to one sailing concept the player must practise.
3. **Define.** Turn the sequence into minigame beats, each with an instruction, a success rule and a score.
4. **Present.** Pair each beat with a line of text: a leaflet note, a light in-game line, or a longer mature version.

The same steps run for every boat. The muleta and the canoa da picada below are worked examples.


## Foundations: concepts, areas and boats

Define these three things once, before any text is mined. They fix the vocabulary that every minigame draws on.


### Sailing concepts

| Concept | Term (PT) | Plain definition | Area |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Close-hauled | bolina | Sailing as close to the wind as the rig allows (about 45°), sails tight. The only way to make ground upwind, by tacking. | Wind |
| Reaching | largo | Wind from the side or from behind the side: sails eased, the fastest and steadiest headings. [Confirm finer PT names with a sailor.] | Wind |

## Step 1: Extract the key passages

Read the source once and tag every sentence that names an action, a place, a condition, a piece of equipment, a number or a quotable line. Each tag becomes raw material for a beat.

| Tag | What to mark | Example from Baldaque da Silva (1895) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Action | A verb the crew performs | Set out the net, bear off, drift, haul in |
| Place | A named spot on the route | River mouth, Trafaria coast, Cacilhas point, Mar da Palha, Entre Cabos |
| Condition | Tide, wind, season, weather | Start of the flood tide; boat lying athwart; a storm that swamps and dismasts her |
| Equipment | Rig, gear, parts | Forward-raked mast, lateen sail, two batelós, six or seven small sails, the tartaranha net |
| Number | Sizes, dates, counts | Royal ban of 9 April 1615; decree of 30 July 1891; two boats left in 1895 |
| Voice | A line worth quoting | "As muletas largam a rede proximo da emboccadura do Tejo, no começo da enchente" |

Three working rules: keep each quote in the original language and translate it separately; record where each tag comes from; mark unclear period terms as [verify] instead of guessing.


## Step 2: Map passages to sailing concepts

Link each tagged action to exactly one concept from the foundations bank above, so every beat teaches one thing. The table below adds what the game shows for each concept. Use the text's own verb to choose the concept.

| Concept | What the player practises | Feedback in the game |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Points of sail | Choose a heading relative to the wind | Wind arrow, speed dial |
| Trim (ease or sheet in) | Match the sail setting to the heading | Trim marker turns green |
| Tacking | Turn through the wind to make ground upwind | Tack count, speed lost in the turn |
| Gybing or passing the yard | Turn with the wind astern | Swing warning, clean or rough pass |
| Reefing or storm sail | Reduce sail as gusts rise | Gust meter, heel angle |
| Lying across wind and current | Hold a beam heading while drifting | Drift line, steadiness bar |
| Approach and dock | Control speed to come alongside | Distance and speed readout |

The sources describe the boats and the work, not sailing technique. Each link from a passage to a concept is therefore a design interpretation, and the design notes should say so.


## Step 3: Define the minigame

Fill one card per minigame. A beat is one action from the text, and the minigame is its beats in the order the source gives them.

| Field | What goes in it | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source | Author, title, year, link | One main source per minigame; list others as support |
| Beats | 3 to 6 actions, in the text's order | One verb and one concept per beat |
| Instruction | The line shown when the beat starts | Imperative, 12 words at most |
| Success rule | What the simulator measures | Heading band, speed range, position or time |
| Soft failure | What happens if the player misses | The beat repeats; the run never ends |
| Numbers | Time limit, goal, full marks | Draft values, tuned in playtests |
| Companion text | The historical line paired with the beat | One sentence, sourced, with a [verify] flag if needed |

Keep a run to about four minutes. Show each line only when its situation arises, so the text follows the sailing and never interrupts it.


## Worked example A: the muleta

Source: A. A. Baldaque da Silva, "Muleta de pesca", *Arte Portugueza* no. 5, 5 May 1895, as reproduced by [Almada Virtual Museum](https://almada-virtual-museum.blogspot.com/2016/09/a-muleta-de-pesca-por-a-baldaque-da.html). The translations and the nautical terms below are my provisional reading.

| Beat | Passage in the source | Concept | Instruction | Success rule (draft) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Set the net | The muletas set the net near the river mouth at the start of the flood tide | Approach and position | Reach the river mouth as the flood begins | Enter the marked zone within 45 s |
| 2. Bear off | They bear off under sail along the Trafaria coast and round the Cacilhas point | Points of sail, trim | Bear away and ease the sails | Heading 120 to 160 degrees off the wind, trim green |
| 3. Lie across | The boat lies athwart, and the small sails offset the after sails | Lying across wind and current | Lie across the current and balance the small sails | Beam heading within 10 degrees for 20 s, balance bar centred |
| 4. Drift the haul | They keep drifting through the Mar da Palha until the haul is complete | Steadiness | Keep her steady until the haul is done | Stay in the channel for 60 s |
| 5. Haul in | They haul in the net and take the fish from the sack | Reward | Haul in the net | Each haul adds to the catch |

Numbers for the run: 4:00, goal of net hauls in the right spot, 16 hauls for full marks. A harder variant follows the text's other route, dragging outside the bar to the cove of Entre Cabos. The storm scene in the article becomes an optional gust event that asks the player to reef.

The passage says nothing about wind direction, so the heading bands in beats 2 and 3 are my interpretation of "bearing off" and "lying athwart".


## Worked example B: the canoa da picada

Source: Victor Moura Vicente, ["Canoa da Picada"](https://mourav.com/canoa-da-picada/). It is a designer's page that cites no sources, so every beat needs a cross-check with the Museu de Marinha before release.

| Beat | Passage in the source | Concept | Instruction | Success rule (draft) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Leave the river | The boats left the Tagus loaded with salt | Points of sail, trim | Sail out of the river with the salt aboard | Reach the open water marker in 60 s |
| 2. Buy the sardines | They bought sardines from fishing boats outside the harbour | Approach and dock | Come alongside slowly and take the sardines | Close under a set speed within a boat length |
| 3. Heavy weather | In strong wind they reefed or hoisted the storm sail, the caixamarim | Reefing and storm sail | Reef before the gust hits | Reduce sail before the gust meter peaks |
| 4. Pass the yard | Passing the yard was done in a light breeze from astern, with great skill and some risk | Gybing or passing the yard | Pass the yard to the other side | Complete the manoeuvre with the wind astern and speed below a limit |
| 5. Reach Lisbon | The load supplied Lisbon's markets, so the boats had to be fast | Points of sail | Run for Lisbon | Arrive within the time limit |

Numbers for the run: 6:00, three sardine loads bought and delivered, 3 loads in time for full marks. The closing line is that the only surviving original, Fatinitza (Setúbal, 1903), is at the Museu de Marinha.

Beat 4 is the strongest link to the sailing drills, because the page describes the manoeuvre in terms a player can feel: light wind, skill, risk.


## Step 4: Present the minigame with text

The same minigame carries three layers of text. Keep the instruction line separate from the history line, so the player can act on the first and read the second when ready.

| Layer | Audience | Length and place | Example from the muleta |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Leaflet | Visitors and families | About 10 lines per boat, printed beside the game | The boat's story: net, rig, route, end of the trade |
| Light in-game | Everyone | One sentence per beat, 20 words at most, shown when the beat starts | "Lie across the current, as the Seixal fishermen did, and balance the small sails." |
| Mature in-game | Adults and students | An optional logbook after the run, switched on by the player | The 1895 quote in Portuguese, its translation, and the context below |

A mature-layer entry for the muleta could read: in 1895 Baldaque da Silva wrote that only two muletas were left. The net that gave them their trade had been banned by royal decree in 1615 for eight years because it damaged the seabed, and a decree of 30 July 1891 extended the ban to the steam-towed trawl.

Three rules keep the layers honest:

- Every line carries its source, and anything unverified carries a [verify] flag until it is checked.
- The mature layer adds context and the original wording, never new gameplay.
- The light layer never explains more than the beat needs, so the sailing stays in front.


## Evidence and evaluation

The literature supports weaving instruction into a light story, but its results on narrative are mixed. I have read only titles and abstracts, so check the findings before citing them.

- [Narrative-Centered Learning Environments](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/288950595_Narrative-Centered_Learning_Environments_A_Story-Centric_Approach_to_Educational_Games), a story-centric approach to educational games.
- [Story-Based Learning: The Impact of Narrative on Learning Experiences and Outcomes](https://public.intellimedia.ncsu.edu/pubmgr/pubdb/pdfs/narrative-its-2008.pdf).
- [Integrating Learning, Problem Solving, and Engagement in Narrative-Centered Learning Environments](https://intellimedia.ncsu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/42/rowe-ijaied-2011.pdf) (Rowe et al., 2011).
- [Why Story Matters: A Review of Narrative in Serious Games](https://tecfa.unige.ch/tecfa/maltt/VIP/Ressources/Articles/naul_liu.pdf).
- [Understanding Learning Content Integration in Games](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S2155684925000090), on putting learning content inside the mechanics.
- [A Mixed-Methods Study of Cultural Heritage Learning through Playing a Serious Game](https://openaccess.bilgi.edu.tr/items/00fc0a98-ea5f-448d-876f-f112d5064ef9).
- From memory, to verify: Habgood and Ainsworth (2011) on intrinsic integration, where the learning content is the game mechanic itself.

A small study can test the three text layers directly. Compare instruction only, instruction plus the light line, and instruction plus the light line and the mature logbook. Measure a short quiz on sailing concepts and on the history before and after play, plus a short engagement questionnaire.

### Numbers for context panels

- 1552: 1,490 river vessels on the Tagus and nearby (maislisboa; getLISBON). Abrantes alone: 180 (100 trading, 80 fishing).
- 1875: 278 boats between Abrantes and Vila Velha de Rodão, 1,360 t in total.
- Upper river loads: 13–20 t (Muge to Abrantes), about 6.7 t (Abrantes to the Sever mouth, rapids).
- Decline: railway reached Barquinha in 1862; steamers from the 1860s; 1951 Vila Franca bridge; dams in the 1950s; 1966 bridge; local quay traffic ended in the early 1970s.
- Surviving boats: Ecomuseu do Seixal holds "Baía do Seixal" (1914), "Gaivotas" (1934) and the varino "Amoroso" (1921).
