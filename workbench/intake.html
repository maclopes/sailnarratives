Concepts

      

      
Each card is one concept: the terms the tool found in different sources that it thinks mean the same thing. If a term is in the wrong card, use Move to… on its row. Group is right confirms the card; Leave out drops it from later steps. Cards you have not decided on are still used. Changing a card's terms sends it back to undecided.

      

    

    

      
Summary of the sources

      
A short reading of the concept table, by type of concept: where the sources agree, where they conflict, where one clarifies another, and what is missing. Rejected concepts are left out. Claude writes it; it is a draft to check against the table.

      

      

    

  


  
  

    
The history base. Claude reads each chosen historical source for practices and events: who did what, with which boat, where, when, under which rule, paraphrased with its page. They are grouped across sources; the tool flags missing and contradictory facts, links each practice to the concepts it exercises, and writes a review sheet for one source. Everything is a draft until you accept it.

Practices and events

      

      
Each card is one practice: passages from different sources that the tool thinks describe the same thing people did. If a passage is in the wrong card, use Move to… on its row. Group is right confirms the card; Leave out drops it from later steps. Cards you have not decided on are still used. Changing a card's passages sends it back to undecided.

      

    

    

      
Review sheet for one source

      
For a historian checking one document: every claim the tool took from it, in paraphrase with its page, and the questions where other sources disagree or facts are missing.

      

      

    

  


  
  

    
Joins each historical practice with the concepts it exercises and what the game can simulate, and proposes an activity: goal, measurable success rule, boat, route, level, length, simplifications and what the game would still need. Proposals are made without seeing the game's own activities; the blind comparison then checks which existing mini-games the method finds on its own. Only processed records are used: no source is read again.

    

    

    
Proposed activities

  


  
  

    
Narrative for the game's mini-games, one at a time. The tool gathers the evidence behind each mini-game (its practices and concepts, with sources and pages), proposes a cast and beats (from your story bible where it covers the mini-game), writes candidate lines in European Portuguese and English that may use only that evidence, and checks every claim. You edit, approve or reject; approved lines export as a line bank for the game. Characters are invented and marked fictional; facts come only from the evidence.
